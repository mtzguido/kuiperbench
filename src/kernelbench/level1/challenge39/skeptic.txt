Skeptic review — KB L2Norm (#39) functional spec
================================================

(a) Does the spec correspond to mathematical row-wise L2
normalisation? YES. The post in Kuiper.KB.L2Norm.fsti is
[l2norm_post b d s s'], which expands to: for every row r < b, the
slice s'[r*d..(r+1)*d] directly approximates
[x / sqrt(sum_j x_j^2)] over the real interpretation of that same
input row. Floating reduction and reciprocal-square-root values are
proof-local implementation details, not existential outputs of the
public contract.

(b) Is the proof genuine?  YES.  grep -n
"assume\|admit\|magic\|admit_smt_queries" turns up nothing in
Kuiper.Spec.L2Norm.{fst,fsti} or Kuiper.KB.L2Norm.fst.  The proof
mirrors Frobenius for the per-row body (sq_step %~ sq_step_r via
a_mul; HReduce yields sumsq %~ frobenius_sumsq_r; the local
rsqrt-approximation bridge connects that result to the mathematical
reciprocal square root; map_gpu lays down the approximating result).
The loop's invariant tracks "rows < vi
already L2-normalised" and "rows ≥ vi unchanged from input"; the
inductive step relies on memcpy_device_to_device''s seq_blit
postcondition.  Two seq_blit≡slice equalities are discharged with
Seq.lemma_eq_intro (pure structural facts).

(c) Edge cases.  b = 0 and d = 0 are ruled out by their positive-size
types.  All-zero rows are excluded by [l2norm_domain]: IEEE
rsqrt(0) followed by multiplication produces infinities/NaNs, which
cannot be related to finite mathematical reals by [%~]. Rows are
processed sequentially in a single for-loop and the invariant proves
the direct result independently for every row.
