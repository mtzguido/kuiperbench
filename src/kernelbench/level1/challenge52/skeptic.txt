Skeptic review — KernelBench L1 #52 (argmin over dim=1)
================================================================

Verified contract
-----------------

The kernel performs one deterministic left-to-right scan per row.  Its pure
specification [row_argmin_partial] starts at index 0 with value +infinity and
replaces the pair (index, value) exactly when [Float32.lt] returns true.  The
challenge postcondition says that every output is the in-bounds index returned
by that exact scan.

The Pulse loop, conversion to [i64], row layout for the (B,D,M) input, and the
bridge from the kernel postcondition to the challenge postcondition are all
checked.  The relevant modules contain no [admit ()], [magic ()], or
[assume pure].

Deliberate limitation
---------------------

Kuiper's abstract floating-point API does not currently expose enough laws to
prove that [lt] selects a semantic minimum, particularly for NaNs.  The former
proof asserted that connection through eight admitted fmax/fmin comparison
lemmas.  Those lemmas have been removed.

Consequently, this contract does not claim that the returned index is the
first mathematical minimum.  It claims the exact comparison scan implemented
by the kernel.  KernelBench's non-NaN test inputs make the operational scan
agree with the PyTorch reference in testing, but that refinement remains
outside the formal contract.

Other notes
-----------

- The precondition [cols < 2^63] justifies conversion of the natural index to
  [i64]; the CUDA bridge's dimensions are much smaller.
- The implementation is one thread per row and prioritizes proof simplicity
  over a block-level parallel reduction.
